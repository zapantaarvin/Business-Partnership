Section 02

Legal Setup & Protection

Structured correctly from day one, you protect your personal assets, your brand, and your investors. This is how you build to last — not just to launch.

Step 1 — Day 1
🏢 Form Your LLC
An LLC separates your personal finances from business liability. Best structure for a small food startup — flexible taxes, simple management, easy co-founder equity splits.
Register your LLC in Virginia (home state) via the VA SCC — ~$100 filing fee
Get an EIN (Employer Identification Number) free at IRS.gov
Open a dedicated business bank account (Relay, Mercury, or Chase Business)
Draft an Operating Agreement defining ownership %, roles, decision-making
Consider a Delaware C-Corp later if you seek VC investment
Step 2 — Week 2
🍫 FDA Food Business Registration
All food facilities that manufacture, process, pack, or hold food must register with the FDA. Your co-manufacturer handles most compliance, but you need your own registration.
Register as a food facility at FDA.gov (free, biennial renewal)
Ensure your co-manufacturer is also FDA-registered and has a Food Safety Plan (FSMA)
Follow FDA labeling rules: Nutrition Facts panel, ingredient list, allergens, net weight
Get a UPC/barcode via GS1 US (~$250/year) for retail distribution
Apply for "Good Manufacturing Practices" (GMP) compliance certification
Step 3 — Month 1
™ Trademark Your Brand
A registered trademark protects your brand name and logo from being copied by competitors. File early — before you launch.
Search USPTO.gov TESS database to confirm your brand name is available
File a trademark application in Class 29 (food products) — $250–$350 per class
Hire a trademark attorney for ~$500–$1,500 to improve approval odds
Also buy your domain (.com) and all social media handles on day 1
File copyright on original artwork and brand identity elements
Step 4 — Month 1
🛡️ Insurance & Risk Management
A single lawsuit or recall can shut down an uninsured food startup. Insurance is non-negotiable in the food industry.
Product Liability Insurance — covers injury/illness claims from your products ($500–$2,000/yr)
General Business Liability Insurance — covers property damage, accidents
Product Recall Insurance — covers cost of pulling product from shelves
Request Certificate of Insurance (COI) from your co-manufacturer
Sign co-packer agreements with indemnification clauses protecting you from their errors
⚠️ Key Risk Protections
Financial Risk
Start lean — use co-manufacturers to avoid $200K+ equipment costs. Limit initial run to 500–1,000 units. Keep 3 months' operating expenses in reserve.
Product Risk
Validate flavors with taste tests BEFORE mass production. Get lab testing (protein content, shelf life, allergen testing) from a certified food lab.
Partnership Risk
Use written contracts for everything — co-manufacturers, distributors, influencers. Include IP ownership clauses, exclusivity terms, and termination conditions.
