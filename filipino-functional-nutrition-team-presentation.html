Who the customer could be

The goal is to start with people who are likely to care about both function and flavor, rather than trying to appeal to everyone immediately.

🏥 Health Care

Busy professionals who need practical, convenient nutrition.

🏋️ Fitness

Consumers who want better flavor than standard protein products.

🇵🇭 Community

Filipino and Asian-American audiences who value cultural connection.

Primary audiences
  • Filipino and Asian-American consumers who want cultural connection
  • Health care professionals who need convenient functional nutrition
  • Fitness-minded consumers who want flavor beyond standard protein products
  • Athletes and creators who can represent the lifestyle authentically
Why these audiences matter early

They are easier to learn from, more likely to give practical feedback, and more likely to help us understand repeat purchase behavior before we expand.

What the customer is really buying

The customer is not just buying macros. They may also be buying convenience, identity, taste novelty, and trust in a brand that feels more human and distinct.

How we want to start with Phase 1

Phase 1 is about validation, not pretending we are already a scaled business.

🧪 Test

Learn which products and flavors people genuinely respond to.

🗣️ Listen

Capture structured feedback instead of guessing what the market wants.

📈 Decide

Use real signals to choose what earns the right to move forward.

Phase 1 objective

Validate whether there is real product-market interest, identify the strongest flavors and product formats, and learn what customers actually respond to.

Phase 1 workstream
  • Research customer preferences and competitors
  • Develop a small set of hero product concepts
  • Run local pilots with targeted groups
  • Collect structured feedback and repeat-order signals
  • Refine pricing, packaging direction, and positioning
What success would look like
  • Clear demand for one or more hero products
  • Strong flavor preference patterns
  • Evidence of repeat interest or reorder intent
  • A narrower, smarter direction for Phase 2

Business and operating model

The point is to build a simple but disciplined framework so we are not improvising everything as we go.

Research

Understand the market

Customer interviews, competitor analysis, flavor validation, and feedback collection.

Product

Develop MVP concepts

Formulation direction, taste testing, nutrition positioning, and packaging thinking.

Operations

Keep the system real

Sourcing, production planning, quality control, and practical execution constraints.

Distribution

Test where demand lives

Hospitals, gyms, Filipino communities, pop-ups, direct orders, and partnerships.

Brand

Create meaning and trust

Storytelling, visuals, messaging, audience relevance, and cultural clarity.

Decision Layer

Review and adapt

Use weekly reporting, insights, and metrics to decide what to continue, change, or stop.

The invitation

The purpose of this pitch is not to pressure anyone. It is to share the vision, show the framework, and give people a chance to decide whether they want to contribute.

What we are really asking from the team
  • Do you believe the idea is worth testing?
  • Do you see a role where your skills could help?
  • Would you want to contribute during Phase 1?
  • If yes, what level of involvement feels realistic?
Why the team matters

A good idea moves faster when the right people help shape it. The business becomes stronger when people contribute from their actual strengths instead of trying to do everything alone.

If someone is interested, the next step is not “join forever.” The next step is to identify what they can help with during Phase 1, what ownership makes sense, and what real contribution would look like. This keeps the opportunity practical, respectful, and aligned with actual interest.
