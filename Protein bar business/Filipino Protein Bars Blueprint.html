How We Make It

Production Process & Kitchen Operations

From rented food-grade kitchen to community evaluation to lab testing — here is every step of building a product the right way.

🏭 Step 1: Rent Food-Grade Kitchen

  • Search for licensed shared-use kitchens (e.g., CloudKitchens, Kitchen United, local commissary kitchens)
  • Verify kitchen has FDA (Food and Drug Administration) food facility registration
  • Sign rental agreement (hourly or monthly, avg $15–$30/hr)
  • Obtain your own food handler's certification (ServSafe)
  • Secure food business liability insurance before first use

🧪 Step 2: Sample Batching

  • Create recipe formulations (3 core flavors: Ube, Pandan, Turon)
  • Document exact ingredient ratios and sourcing
  • Produce small batches (25–50 units per trial run)
  • Track costs per unit for each batch
  • Photograph and video every batch for content creation

👥 Step 3: Community Evaluation

  • Host 2–3 tasting events at Filipino community events or churches
  • Use structured feedback form (taste, texture, sweetness, price willingness)
  • Target 50+ evaluators across Filipino and non-Filipino testers
  • Iterate recipe based on feedback (3 rounds minimum)
  • Film reactions — authentic community content is powerful marketing

🔬 Step 4: Lab Testing

  • Use certified lab: Eurofins, SGS, or Covance for nutritional analysis
  • Verify protein, carb, fat, and micronutrient content
  • Test for allergens (nuts, gluten, dairy)
  • Shelf-life stability testing (accelerated aging studies)
  • Retain lab reports — required for FDA labeling compliance
Packaging Development
Design
Use Canva AI or hire a Filipino designer on Upwork. Include brand colors (deep navy, gold, red), Filipino sun symbol, Tagalog tagline, clean nutrition facts panel.
Printing Partners
Noissue (eco-friendly, min 250 units), Sticker Mule (labels, min 50), Pakible (custom wrappers). Budget $0.10–$0.35/unit for labels at scale.
FDA (Food and Drug Administration) Requirements
Nutrition Facts label (FDA format), ingredient list, net weight, allergen statement, manufacturer address, and UPC (Universal Product Code / barcode) from GS1 US ($250 one-time fee).
Prototype Testing
Print 10–20 sample wrappers before full run. Check readability, color accuracy, seal integrity, and moisture barrier performance.
Eco Mission
Compostable or recyclable wrappers align with your faith-based stewardship values. Highlight on packaging: "Eco-conscious. Community-funded."
Scale Up
At 1,000+ units/month, transition to co-packer (PartnerSlate, YouBar, EMPWR) with full turnkey production including packaging and fulfillment.

Protection & Structure

Legal Setup & Partnership Strategy

Doing business the right way means protecting your team, your brand, and your mission with the proper legal structure from day one.

🏛️ Business Formation

  1. 1Form an LLC (Limited Liability Company) in your state (avg $50–$500 filing fee). Protects personal assets — if sued, only business assets are at risk, not your personal savings.
  2. 2Draft an Operating Agreement defining team roles, profit splits, and decision-making process. Do this before money is involved.
  3. 3Obtain EIN (Employer Identification Number) — free from IRS.gov. Required for bank accounts and taxes.
  4. 4Open a dedicated business bank account. Never mix personal and business funds.
  5. 5Get business liability insurance ($500–$1,500/yr) and product liability insurance for food businesses.

📋 Food Business Compliance

  1. 1Register as an FDA Food Facility (free at FDA.gov). Required before selling packaged food products.
  2. 2Follow FDA labeling rules: Nutrition Facts panel, ingredient list, allergen statement, net weight, manufacturer address.
  3. 3Obtain GS1 UPC (Universal Product Code / barcode) registration ($250/yr for up to 10 products). Required for retail shelves and Amazon listings.
  4. 4Register your brand trademark at USPTO.gov ($250/class). USPTO stands for U.S. Patent and Trademark Office — this legally protects your brand name and logo so no one else can copy it.
  5. 5Check state cottage food laws vs. commercial kitchen requirements for your production volume.
