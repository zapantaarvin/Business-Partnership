Phase By Phase

A practical roadmap to go from idea to proof

Phase 1

Clarify the concept

Define the mission, target customer, product thesis, and what makes this culturally meaningful and commercially different.

Phase 2

Prototype products

Develop a small set of bars and drinks with Filipino-inspired flavors and a clear functional positioning.

Phase 3

Test with a small circle

Test with friends, family, health care workers, and active communities to learn which flavors, formats, and claims resonate fastest.

Phase 4

Refine operations

Tighten formulation, sourcing, shelf life, packaging, pricing, and repeatability before broader selling.

Phase 5

Launch a focused pilot

Start with local pilots through gyms, health facilities, Filipino markets, pop-ups, or direct online drops instead of a large rollout.

Phase 6

Grow with proof

Expand only after seeing consistent demand, reliable execution, and a strong brand story people repeat on their own.

Risk Reality

What we would need to manage early

Consistency

Taste, texture, mix quality, and product performance need to stay reliable as production scales.

Inventory

Specialty ingredients and packaging components can be harder to forecast, especially if demand grows faster than expected.

Supply chain

Ingredient sourcing, copacker or manufacturer availability, and cost volatility can affect margins and launch timing.

Capacity

A strong early response can create pressure on formulation, production, fulfillment, and founder bandwidth if systems are not ready.

Brand drift

Trying to appeal to everyone too early can blur the identity that makes the concept special.

Unit economics

Even a strong product will struggle if formulation cost, manufacturing, shipping, and retail margin requirements do not support the model.
