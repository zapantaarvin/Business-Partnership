1. Meeting Purpose

This section keeps the discussion focused and makes the intention clear from the beginning.

Primary Objective: Learn from Maico’s business experience before taking major steps toward opening a laundry shop. The goal is not to pitch a finalized plan, but to receive guidance, understand what to validate first, and avoid common beginner mistakes.

What We Want to Learn

  • How Maico started in business
  • What challenges he faced early
  • How he balanced risk, time, and money
  • What he would do differently if starting again
  • How to think about partnerships and accountability
  • How to validate an idea before investing capital

What We Are Not Doing Yet

  • We are not presenting a final business plan
  • We are not committing to a location yet
  • We are not making financial decisions yet
  • We are not assuming the laundry shop idea is already validated

3. Group Context & Business Idea

This explains who we are and why we are exploring business.

Our Group

Who We Are

We are a group of four men who meet biweekly for Bible study. Over time, our conversations have also included personal growth, career development, finances, leadership, and the possibility of building something outside of our full-time jobs.

Initial Idea

Laundry Shop Business

Our initial business idea is to explore opening and operating a laundry shop. We are still at the early learning stage. We have not committed money, selected a location, or finalized a plan. We want to first learn from business owners and people with real experience.

Important Framing: We should present this as an early exploration, not as a fully validated business. This keeps the discussion humble and opens the door for honest mentor feedback.

4. Draft Mission, Vision, and Guiding Principles

These are draft statements. They can be refined after the mentor discussion.

Mission

To build a reliable, clean, and efficient laundry service that serves the local community while helping our team learn business ownership, operations, and financial discipline.

Vision

To grow from one well-managed laundry location into a sustainable business model that can eventually produce consistent income, create jobs, and open opportunities for future ventures.

Purpose

To learn business through disciplined execution, shared accountability, and wise decision-making while building something that can provide long-term value.

Guiding Principles

  • Learn first, invest later: Validate the idea before spending serious money.
  • Clear roles prevent conflict: Define responsibilities before launching.
  • Small steps create progress: Break big goals into manageable tasks.
  • Track everything: Decisions, costs, assumptions, risks, and action items should be documented.
  • Protect relationships: Friendship and faith-based connection should not replace business agreements.

Phase 1 Validation Page: Research & Decision-Making

Use this page to validate real costs, real effort, real risks, and whether the group should move forward. This phase should be treated as a decision gate, not a commitment to start the business.

Phase 1 Goal: Determine whether the laundry business idea is financially realistic, operationally manageable, and worth moving into Phase 2. The group should only move forward if there is either a strong existing deal or a strong validated location, and the team shows early alignment and follow-through.
Validate

Real Costs

Confirm the true startup and operating costs before committing money. This includes equipment, rent, utilities, permits, maintenance, supplies, insurance, accounting, and emergency reserves.

Validate

Real Effort

Confirm how much weekly work the business will require. Do not assume it is passive income. Identify what can be automated and what will still require people, time, and accountability.

Validate

Real Risks

Identify the risks that could make this business fail or become stressful, such as weak location, machine breakdowns, high utilities, lease problems, partnership issues, and low customer demand.

Only Move Forward If: The group finds a good existing laundromat deal OR a strong validated location, and the team agreement is solid enough to justify moving into Phase 2.

Decision Criteria

Real Cost Checklist

Startup Costs

  • Washer and dryer purchase or lease
  • Equipment delivery and installation
  • Plumbing, electrical, gas, and ventilation work
  • Lease deposit and first months of rent
  • Permits, inspections, and business registration
  • Renovation, signage, furniture, carts, folding tables
  • Payment system, Wi-Fi, cameras, website, POS
  • Opening inventory and cleaning supplies
  • Insurance and professional fees
  • 20–30% contingency buffer

Monthly Operating Costs

  • Rent
  • Water, electricity, gas
  • Internet and phone
  • Insurance
  • Repairs and maintenance
  • Cleaning supplies
  • Payroll or contractor help
  • Accounting/bookkeeping
  • Marketing
  • Loan payments, if financed

Owner Interview Script

  • How did you get started in the laundry business?
  • Did you buy an existing location or build from scratch?
  • What did you underestimate when you first started?
  • What are your biggest monthly expenses?
  • How often do machines break or need service?
  • How much time does the business require weekly?
  • What makes a location good or bad?
  • What would you check before signing a lease?
  • What advice would you give a group of first-time business owners?
  • If you were starting again, what would you do differently?

Phase 1 Final Decision Gate

Decision Meaning When to Choose This Next Step
GO Move to Phase 2 Costs are realistic, demand is strong, risks are manageable, and the team is reliable. Start formal team agreement, LLC research, and business foundation.
RESEARCH MORE Extend Phase 1 There is potential, but major data is missing or uncertain. Assign missing research and revisit decision in 1–2 weeks.
STOP / PIVOT Do not proceed Costs are too high, demand is weak, risks are too large, or team commitment is weak. Document lessons learned and consider a smaller or different business idea.
