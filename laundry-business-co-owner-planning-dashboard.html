1. Purpose of This Co-Owner Discussion

This page is meant to help the group think clearly before moving too fast.

Primary Goal: Decide whether the group is ready to begin Phase 1 research together. This is not yet the stage for forming the LLC, buying equipment, signing a lease, or committing money. The focus is alignment, research, and discipline.
Why We Are Meeting

Business Exploration

We want to explore whether a laundry shop can be a realistic business opportunity. The goal is to learn, validate, and make wise decisions before investing money.

Deeper Purpose

Leadership & Growth

This is not only about money. We want to grow as disciplined, financially educated, God-centered men who can lead well, support our families, mentor others, and help the community.

Important Reminder: Friendship and brotherhood are valuable, but business still requires clarity. Roles, money, time, ownership, accountability, and exit plans must be discussed early to protect both the business and the relationship.

3. Co-Owner Alignment Questions

Before assigning tasks, make sure everyone is honest about motivation, time, money, and expectations.

Personal Motivation

  • Why are you interested in starting a business?
  • Are you more interested in learning, income, ownership, or leadership?
  • What would make this worth your time?
  • What would make you step away?

Time & Capacity

  • How many hours per week can you realistically contribute?
  • Can you attend recurring meetings?
  • Can you complete tasks by agreed deadlines?
  • Are there seasons where your availability will be low?

Financial Readiness

  • Are you comfortable discussing money openly?
  • Are you willing to contribute capital if the idea is validated?
  • What amount would be realistic without hurting personal finances?
  • Are you comfortable with loans or personal guarantees?

Partnership Expectations

  • How should ownership be split?
  • Should ownership be based on money, work, or both?
  • What happens if someone stops contributing?
  • How should disagreements be handled?

7. Team Rhythm & Execution System

This keeps the group organized without overcomplicating Phase 1.

Weekly / Biweekly Meeting Format

  • Prayer / opening: 2–3 minutes
  • Wins: What moved forward?
  • Task updates: What was completed?
  • Blockers: What is slowing us down?
  • Discussion: What decisions need input?
  • Assignments: Who owns what next?
  • Closing: Confirm due dates and next meeting.

Operating Rules

  • Everyone owns at least one task during Phase 1.
  • Tasks must have an owner and due date.
  • If someone is blocked, they communicate early.
  • Decisions should be documented, not just discussed.
  • No major spending without group agreement.
  • Do not move to Phase 2 until Phase 1 evidence is reviewed.

8. Phase 1 Decision Gate

At the end of Phase 1, the group should decide: go, research more, or stop/pivot.

Decision Meaning When to Choose This Next Step
GO Move to Phase 2 Costs are realistic, demand is strong, risks are manageable, and the team showed reliable follow-through. Start formal partnership discussion, legal research, operating agreement topics, and business foundation.
RESEARCH MORE Extend Phase 1 The idea has potential, but important information is missing or uncertain. Assign missing research and revisit the decision in 1–2 weeks.
STOP / PIVOT Do not proceed yet Costs are too high, demand is weak, risks are too large, or the team is not aligned. Document lessons learned and explore a smaller or different opportunity.
Red Flag: If the team cannot consistently complete research tasks in Phase 1, that is a warning sign. Running a real business will require much more discipline than researching one.

9. Topics That Must Be Discussed Before Phase 2

These do not all need to be finalized today, but they should be discussed before serious commitment.

Ownership & Money
  • Will ownership be equal or based on contribution?
  • How much capital is each person expected to contribute?
  • What happens if one person contributes more money?
  • How will profits be distributed?
  • Will profits be reinvested first before owners take distributions?
  • Who controls the business bank account?
Roles & Workload
  • Who is responsible for operations?
  • Who is responsible for finances?
  • Who is responsible for marketing?
  • Who handles vendor communication?
  • What happens if someone does not complete assigned work?
  • Should sweat equity count toward ownership?
Decision-Making
  • Which decisions require unanimous agreement?
  • Which decisions can be made by role owner?
  • How will disagreements be resolved?
  • Who has final say during urgent situations?
  • How will decisions be documented?
Exit Plan
  • What happens if someone wants to leave?
  • Can ownership be sold to an outsider?
  • Does the group get first right to buy back ownership?
  • How will the business be valued if someone exits?
  • What happens if someone stops contributing but wants to keep ownership?
